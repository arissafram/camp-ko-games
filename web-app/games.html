More Games

With these tools in your back pocket, you’ll have everything you need to build all kinds of games and projects. Here are a few examples to inspire what you create next.

How do I start?

First, describe what your game does in one sentence.

Next, start your pseudocode. Before you write JavaScript, think through what appears on the screen, what the player does, what your game needs to remember, what makes things happen, and what happens next.

You don’t need to know how to build every part yet. Start by figuring out what needs to happen.

What if I get stuck?

Getting stuck is part of coding! Try to figure out what’s not working before you try to fix it.

Read the error. Check your code. Try changing one thing. Use the Concepts page. Ask a friend. Lean on your teammates—we’re here to help each other.

Each game page also includes the finished code. Use it as a reference when you’re really stuck—but don’t copy/paste! Look for the part you need, figure out how it works, then try it in your own code.

What if I don’t finish?

That’s okay! In engineering, things are rarely truly finished—we iterate.

Think of your game as an MVP: Minimum Viable Product. First, make it work. Then make it yours. If you have time, add something extra.

Each game page will show you what the basic version needs. Everything after that is up to you—and you can keep building long after this week is over.

Can I use AI?

Yes, but… AI should help you think, it shouldn’t think for you.

Try

  • “I’m learning JavaScript. Don’t give me the answer, but point me in the right direction.”
  • “Can you explain why this isn’t working without fixing it for me?”
  • “Can you explain this JavaScript like I’m five? Keep it short.”
  • “What should I investigate next?”

🔮 Magic 8 Ball

A game where the player asks a question and gets a randomly selected response.

Source Code ↗

Tasks

  • Your game has a name.
  • If your game needs instructions, write them.
  • Your game has your own pizzazz.
  • Your game responds when the player asks a question.
  • Your game has a variety of possible responses.

Plan Before You Start

  • How does the player ask a question? Do they type it in or ask it out loud?
  • What action makes the Magic 8 Ball respond?
  • What kinds of answers can it give?
  • Where does the answer appear on the screen?
  • What happens when the player asks another question?

Challenges!

  • Add different answer modes. Let the player choose something like Pirate, Optimistic, or Dramatic before asking.
  • Make certain questions or words trigger special responses.
  • What else could you add?

💰 Hidden Treasure

A game where the player clicks hiding spots to search for a randomly hidden treasure.

Source Code ↗

Tasks

  • Your game has a name.
  • If your game needs instructions, write them.
  • Your game has your own pizzazz.
  • The treasure is hidden in a random spot each game.
  • Clicking a spot reveals whether it has the treasure.
  • Your game tells the player when they’ve found the treasure.

Plan Before You Start

  • How many hiding spots will your game have?
  • How does the game randomly choose which spot has the treasure?
  • What happens when the player clicks a spot with no treasure?
  • What happens when the player clicks the spot with the treasure?
  • Once a spot is opened, can the player click it again?
  • What happens when the player wants to play again?

Challenges!

  • Count how many spots the player checked before finding the treasure.
  • Use Math.random to hide the gold in a new place each time.
  • What else could you add?

🪨 Rock Paper Scissors

A game where the player chooses rock, paper, or scissors and competes against a randomly chosen computer move.

Source Code ↗

Tasks

  • Your game has a name.
  • If your game needs instructions, write them.
  • Your game has your own pizzazz.
  • The player can choose a move.
  • The computer randomly chooses a move.
  • Your game figures out and displays the winner.

Plan Before You Start

  • How does the player choose rock, paper, or scissors?
  • How will the computer choose?
  • How does the game know which move wins?
  • What happens when both players choose the same thing?
  • Where will you show each choice and the result?
  • What happens when the player wants to play again?

Challenges!

  • Keep score across multiple rounds and make the first to a certain score the winner.
  • Invent new moves and decide what beats what. Rock, Paper, Scissors, Lizard, Spock? Or something completely your own.
  • What else could you add?

  • What else could you add?

⏳ Clicker

A game where every click adds to your score.

Source Code ↗

Tasks

  • Your game has a name.
  • If your game needs instructions, write them.
  • Your game has your own pizzazz.
  • Clicking increases your score.
  • Your score is always visible on the screen.
  • There’s a way to reset your score.

Plan Before You Start

  • What should happen every time the player clicks?
  • Does the score keep going forever, or does the game end?
  • What happens when the player resets?
  • Where does the score appear on the screen?
  • What could make clicking feel more fun or satisfying?

Challenges!

  • Add a timer so the game ends after 10 seconds.
  • Give two players their own button and score, and see who gets the highest score.
  • What else could you add?
