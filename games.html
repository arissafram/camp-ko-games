Games

Choose a game that sounds fun to build and play. A game doesn’t need to have a winner—or even traditional rules. Look through the options, think about what interests you, and imagine how you could make it your own!

How do I start?

Start with pseudocode. Before you write JavaScript, think through what appears on the screen, what the player does, what your game needs to remember, what makes things happen, and what happens next.

You don’t need to know how to build every part yet. Start by figuring out what needs to happen.

Choose your game

Game The Idea You’ll Use
🔮 Magic 8 Ball Ask a question and get a random response arrays, randomness, input, events
🏎️ Race Cars Move a racer to the finish line with the keyboard keyboard events, variables, conditionals
🧠 Memory Flip cards and find matching pairs arrays, events, conditionals, timers
🪨 Rock Paper Scissors Choose a move and play against the computer arrays, randomness, conditionals
🤯 The Impossible Game A game where the rules are hidden or constantly changing functions, DOM changes, conditionals, timers

What if I get stuck?

Getting stuck is part of coding! Try to figure out what’s not working before you try to fix it.

Read the error. Check your code. Try changing one thing. Use the Concepts page. Ask a teammate what they tried. Ask for help.

Each game page also includes the finished code. Use it as a reference when you’re really stuck—but don’t copy/paste! Look for the part you need, figure out how it works, then try it in your own code.

What if I don’t finish?

That’s okay! In engineering, things are rarely truly finished—we iterate.

Think of your game as an MVP: Minimum Viable Product. First, make it work. Then make it yours. If you have time, add something extra.

Each game page will show you what the basic version needs. Everything after that is up to you—and you can keep building long after this week is over.

Can I use AI?

Yes—but… AI should help you think, not think for you.

Try

  • “I’m learning JavaScript. Don’t give me the answer, but point me in the right direction.”
  • “Can you explain why this isn’t working without fixing it for me?”
  • “Can you explain this JavaScript like I’m five? Keep it short.”
  • “What should I investigate next?”

🔮 Magic 8 Ball

A game where the player asks a question and gets a randomly selected response.

Source Code ↗

Tasks

  • Your game has a name.
  • If your game needs instructions, write them.
  • Your game has your own pizzazz.
  • Your game responds when the player asks a question.
  • Your game has a variety of possible responses.

Plan Before You Start

  • How does the player ask a question? Do they type it in or ask it out loud?
  • What action makes the Magic 8 Ball respond?
  • What kinds of answers can it give?
  • Where does the answer appear on the screen?
  • What happens when the player asks another question?

Challenges!

  • Add different answer modes. Let the player choose something like Pirate, Optimistic, or Dramatic before asking.
  • Make certain questions or words trigger special responses.
  • What else could you add?

🏎️ Race Cars

A racing game where players use the keyboard to move their racers toward a finish line.

Source Code ↗

Tasks

  • Your game has a name.
  • If your game needs instructions, write them.
  • Your game has your own pizzazz.
  • Each player can control their racer.
  • Your game has a start and a finish.
  • Your game tells the players who won.

Plan Before You Start

  • What are you racing? Cars? Animals? Spaceships? Something else?
  • Which key controls each racer?
  • What happens each time a player presses their key?
  • How does the game know when a racer reaches the finish line?
  • What happens when someone wins?
  • How does a new race start?

Challenges!

  • Add obstacles or special spaces that move a racer forward or backward.
  • Make it best of three and keep track of who wins each race.
  • What else could you add?

🧠 Memory

A card game where the player flips over cards and tries to find all of the matching pairs.

Source Code ↗

Tasks

  • Your game has a name.
  • If your game needs instructions, write them.
  • Your game has your own pizzazz.
  • Cards can be flipped over and hidden again.
  • Your game recognizes matching pairs.
  • Your game tells the player when they’ve found every match.

Plan Before You Start

  • What will be on your cards?
  • What do the cards look like when they’re hidden?
  • What happens when the player flips over one card? What about a second?
  • How does the game know if two cards match?
  • What happens to cards that don’t match?
  • How does the game know when there are no matches left to find?

Challenges!

  • Count how many turns it takes to find every pair.
  • Add a timer so the player can try to beat their fastest time.
  • What else could you add?

🪨 Rock Paper Scissors

A game where the player chooses rock, paper, or scissors and competes against a randomly chosen computer move.

Source Code ↗

Tasks

  • Your game has a name.
  • If your game needs instructions, write them.
  • Your game has your own pizzazz.
  • The player can choose a move.
  • The computer randomly chooses a move.
  • Your game figures out and displays the winner.

Plan Before You Start

  • How does the player choose rock, paper, or scissors?
  • How will the computer choose?
  • How does the game know which move wins?
  • What happens when both players choose the same thing?
  • Where will you show each choice and the result?
  • What happens when the player wants to play again?

Challenges!

  • Keep score across multiple rounds and make the first to a certain score the winner.
  • Invent new moves and decide what beats what. Rock, Paper, Scissors, Lizard, Spock? Or something completely your own.
  • What else could you add?

🤯 The Impossible Game

A game where the player tries to win without knowing all of the rules in advance.

Source Code ↗

Tasks

  • Your game has a name.
  • Decide what the player is trying to accomplish.
  • Your game has your own pizzazz.
  • Your game responds to what the player does.
  • Your game has rules the player has to figure out.
  • There is a way to win.

Plan Before You Start

  • What does the player think they’re supposed to do?
  • What is the actual rule?
  • What happens when the player does the wrong thing?
  • How does the player figure out what to try next?
  • Do the rules stay the same or change?
  • How does the player eventually win?

Challenges!

  • Make the rules change as the player gets further into the game.
  • Add a secret interaction or hidden way to win that the player has to discover.
  • What else could you add?
